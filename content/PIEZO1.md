PIEZO1 is a mechanically activated ion channel that opens up in response to:
1. membrane stretch and tension
2. shear stress/fluid flow
3. pressure/osmotic stress
Structurally, PIEZO-1 is a curved, propeller-like blade that flattens in response to membrane tension, opening the pore and enabling ion permeation.

### Primary Biophysical Roles
- non-selective cation channel highly permeable to Ca<sup>2+</sup> and opens to mechanical force
- transduces force into Ca<sup>2+</sup> influx, local Ca<sup>2+</sup> nanodomains and downstream signalling
- this results in changes in gene expression, metabolism, cytoskeleton, polarity and cell fate

### Physiological roles

PIEZO1 acts as a force sensor, that links mechanical environment to specific functions:
1. Vasculature and heart - senses shear stress and stretch in endothelial and smooth muscle cells
2. Blood cells - senses tension from cell-cell/matrix adhesion, flow, pressure, stiffness
3. Skeleton and muscle - detects load, shear, contraction-related forces
4. Immune cells and others - mediates stiffness sensing and polarisation in macrophages

###  Mechanotransduction

Piezo1 channels reliably elevate intracellular Ca<sup>2+</sup> in a variety of cases. Mechanical cues such as stretch, shear, acoustic force activate PIEZO1, which causes robust Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers, and these responses are completely lost on Piezo1 inhibition. In epithelial cells like keratocytes, pulling forces (~pN) on E-cadherin or traction forces at focal adhesions trigger localised Piezo1-dependent Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers. 


In mechanosensation, PIEZO1 functions as a **“force‑through‑membrane” sensor**: it directly detects changes in membrane tension/curvature and rapidly converts them into Ca²⁺‑based signals that reprogram cell behaviour, enabling tissues to adapt structure and function to mechanical loads.

## PIEZO1 and Calcium dynamics

A main feature is the formation of Ca<sup>2+</sup> nanodomains.

1. Mechanical activation or chemical agonists (e.g., Yoda1) open PIEZO1, causing **rapid Ca²⁺ influx** that forms localized high‑Ca²⁺ “micro/nanodomains” at the plasma membrane.
2. These nanodomains can trigger **Ca²⁺‑induced Ca²⁺ release (CICR)** from the ER and controlled Ca²⁺ uptake into mitochondria, supporting oscillations and intercellular Ca²⁺ waves while limiting overload.
