[Link to paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-019-0514-3)

Just like Piezo1 plays an important role in transducing outside-in mechanical forces, it is also activated by the several mechanical forces generated by the cell itself - e.g., myosin II generated tractional forces for probing the stifffness of the ECM. In some cells the activation of Piezo1 requires these cell-generated forces, but the mechanisms of activation are essentially unknown.

Patch clamp assays drastically affects the native environment of Piezo1, disrupt cytoskeletal dynamics, and do not provite spatial information - hence calcium flux (through the channel) was imaged.

It is already known that traction forces elicit calcium flickers from endogenous Piezo1 channels in the absence of external forces. The spatial regulation of Piezo1 b traction forces is measured ny imaging Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers, traction forces, and Piezo1 localisation. 

---

>[!Check] Piezo1 generates Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers

TIRF in WT and Piezo1<sup>-/-</sup> mutant hNSPCs have shown that Piezo1 activation causes Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers. Almost 82% of Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers at the cell-substrate interface in a variety of cell types derive from Piezo1 activity. 

>[!Question] How do you determine the localisation of Piezo1-dependent Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers relative to hotspots of traction forces?

Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers are visualised by imaging Ca<sup>2+</sup>  influx through the channel using TIRFM. The signals are processed to increase the signal to noise ration of the signals of interest - and then passed through a clustering algorithm to detect flickering events. Once events are detected, a 2D Gaussian curve is fit to every event in the movie to determine the localisation of each flicker event. *This allows one to locate the individual ion channels or the centre of mass of a group of ion channels that cause the flicker.* 

---

>[!Check] Piezo1 activity is enriched in regions with high traction forces

Cell shape determines the location of traction force generation, while the cell size determines their magnitude. By micropatterning substrates and controlling the shape and size of the cells, the **spatial pattern and magnitudes of the traction forces were varied**. 

Cells were constrained to a square shape - where the traction forces are highest at the vertices > edges> middle, and Piezo1 Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers were images to see if they follow a similar dependence on cell shape and size. Cells seeded on square fibronectin islands yield a single square cell per island - tthe square shapes were of three different sizes to generate different magnitudes of traction forces. 

To confirm that the cells exhibited the expected shape and cytoskeleton, actin, focal adhesions and nuclei were observed. Cells on larger islands displayed greater numbers of, and longer stress fibers.

Piezo1 Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers were imaged in live cells adhering to the three sizes of islands - and the following results:

1. Flicker frequencies scaled with cell size - with larger cells having more flickers.
2. Flicker amplitudes scaled with cell size, with larger cells (having larger traction forces) having larger flickers.
3. In medium and large cells (small ones were excluded because of smallers flicker frequency), flickers were localised mostly at the corners and edges.
4. Flickers in the corner and edge were of larger amplitude than the moddle flickers.

Thus, it was found that Piezo1 Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers enrich in regions of higher traction forces, and that flicker frequency and amplitude scales with cell spread area.

---

>[!Check] Piezo1 Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers are spatially correlated with higher traction forces

Traction forces and Piezo1 flikcers were measured in the same cell. A FRET-based molecular tension sensor was used to measure cellular traction forces. Traction forces generated bu the cell are communicated to the MTS via integrin-MTS attachments - these forces cause extension of the peptide spring - and a reduction in FRET efficiency.

A high FRET index indicates low traction force, and a low FRET index indicates high traction force.

Overlaying maps of the traction forces and Piezo1 Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers demonstrated their correlation. On calculating the distance of Piezo1 Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers from their nearest force-producing region, the flikcer sites were closer to high-force adhesion than would be expected by chance. This proved that Piezo1 Ca<sup>2+</sup> flikcer location is spatially correlaed with higher traction forces.

---

>[!Check] Piezo1 channels diffuse over the surface of the cell


The traction forces produced by myosin II are communicated via actin filaments to focal adhesions that attach to the substrate. The existence of Piezo1 Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers predominantly in the vicinity of focal adhesions suggests:
1. Piezo1 channels are localised to focal adhesions where traction forces are transmitted to the substrate.
2. Piezo1 channels are present all over the surface, but are only activated by traction forces near force-producing adhesions.

To distinguish between the two possibilities, the localisation of endogenous Piezo1 was observed, and the distribution was found to be all over the cell. Individual Piezo1 channel puncta were mobile, and their trajectories show a similar diffusion coefficient to other proteins. This largely proves the second hypothesis.

---

>[!Check] Myosin II phosphorylation activates Piezo1

Myosin II hydrolyses ATP to generate mechanical force that is transmitted to the ECM. Inhibition of myosin II by blebbistatin inhibits Piezo1 Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers, establishing that **force generation by myosin II is required for Piezo1 Ca<sup>2+</sup> flicker activity**. 

Myosin II is phosphorylated by two kinases - ROCK and MLCK, that converts it into an active form. ROCK phosphorylates myosin II in the centre of cells, while MLCK phosphorylates myosin II in the periphery. Inhibiting ROCK had no effect on Piezo1 ativity, while inhibiting MLCK inhibited Piezo1 Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers. This was consistent with the observation that

>[!bug] **Piezo1 Ca<sup>2+</sup> flickers are more often observed in the periphery of cells.**

Calyculin A failed to increase flicker activity in the absence of external Ca2+, indicating that Ca2+ influx across the plasma membrane is required. 

Can changing the distribution of MLCK or overexpressing MLCK in different regions change the spatial distribution of Piezo1 then?
