The underlying principles guiding the subcellular distribution of Piezo1 remains unknown. The large size and curved blade-like architecture of Piezo1 trimers make them directly sensitive to membrane tension. However, there have been additional studies that indicate a cytoskeletal role in the activation of Piezo1 channels, even though the presence of direct interacton with Piezo1 is not proven yet. The cytoskeleton can drastically affect the extent of membrane tension propagation, thus indirectly controlling Piezo1 activation via the lipid bilayer.

> [!Example] Preferential subcellular distribution of Piezo1 has been found in many cases:
> 
> 1. Piezo1 localises towards the rear of migrating keratinocytes and controls speed of wound healing.
>  2. Piezo1 enriches in subcellular structures important for mechanotransduction, like focal adhesion sites.
>  3. Piezo1 localises to the intercellular bridge during cytokinesis.

It is popularly hypothesised that **membrane curvature** may play a role in regulating this cellular distribution. In fact, several pieces of evidence have been found to support this:

> [!Example] Evidence supporting membrane curvature as a modulator:
> 1. Piezo1 trimers form nanoscale invaginations in a simple liposomal system.
> 2. While locally stretching membrane tether activates Piezo1, Ca<sup>2+</sup> influx initiates at the cell-tether attachment point where memb. tension is high. However, Ca<sup>2+</sup> initiation sites are noticeably absent from the highly tensed membrane tether itself.
> 3. Force dependent Ca<sup>2+</sup> signals in filopodia are independent of Piezo1 channels - since filopodia are highly curved, it would make sense that Piezo1 channels are actually **absent from highly curved membrane tension**.

---

### Experiments

In the first experiment, human Piezo1 was co-expressed with GPI anchored mCherry in HeLa cells (to label membrane). It was found that:
1. Piezo1 traffics well to the plasma membrane.
2. Piezo1 signal was noticeably missing on filopodia.
3. On normalising the Piezo1 density on filopodia to the density on a flat cell body
, the normalised density of Piezo1 in filopodia was almost zero in a variety of cell types, across endogenous expression and over expression treatments.

---

> [!Question] Is the exclusion of Piezo1 specific to filopodia, and dp the specialised actin-rich structures in filopodia influence these exclusions?

Hence, the next experiments focused on membrane tethers that geometrically mimic filopodia but lack specific actin-based structures when freshly pulled.

It was found that there is no significant difference between Piezo1's sorting on tethers and on filopodia - *high membrane curvature is common to both*.
To test if changing membrane curvature changes Piezo1 sorting, two tests were done:

| **Test**                                             | **Result**                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Does S<sub>filo</sub> change with filopodium radius? | **No dependence**, unlike other membrane proteins with radius-dependent sorting.                                              |
| Does S<sub>tether</sub> vary with tether relaxation? | **No dependence** - varying tether radii by pulling them to different tensions did not affect the extent of Piezo1 exclusion. |

Since all the structures, natural filopodia and artifical tethers had radii <50nm, and Piezo1 was excluded in all cases, it was hypothesised that 
1. Piezo1 might be able to sort to slighly less curved membranes (with curvature radii >50nm), or 
2. Piezo1 might be strongly attached to the cortical cytoskeleton, preventing it from moving onto membrane protrusions.

To test these hypotheses, **stable membrane blebs** were generated by depolymerising actin. Bleb membrane contained Piezo1 signal, but not significantly enriched relative to cell body. 

1. On pulling tethers from tense blebs (that have high membrane tension), **negligible Piezo1 fluorescence was observed even in the absence of cytoskeleton** - a terrible blow to the hypothesis that Piezo1 sorting is obstructed by cortical cytoskeleton tethering.
2. Due to low membrane tension on floppy bleb membranes, wider tethers could be pulled - and **Piezo1 was no longer excluded**. Thus the exclusion is not due to cytoskeletal anchoring but due to membrane curvature.

---

>[!Question] Could membrane tension, instead of membrane curvature, be responsible for Piezo1 sorting?

 Membrane tension equilibrates across a continuous membrane (bleb + tether) within less than a second.
- They imaged tethers >1 minute after pulling, so the system is in equilibrium.

- In a single equilibrated tether-bleb system, any tension effect would be uniform, yet Piezo1 is sorted: depleted from the thin tether, present on the bleb. This local difference in concentration between two connected, equilibrated membranes strongly suggests that **curvature directly modulates Piezo1’s distribution**, not tension.

---

>[!Check] Finding: Yoda1 flattens Piezo1 and changes its sorting

Since Yoda1 makes Piezo1 flatter (smaller C<sub>0</sub>​), the model predicted that **Piezo1 should become less depleted from filopodia**.

- **Piezo1 signal on filopodia significantly increased** after Yoda1 treatment, while filopodia radii stayed the same.
- The sorting coefficient S<sub>filo</sub> now showed a **positive correlation with filopodia radius**. This means that on thicker filopodia (less curvature), Piezo1 enters even more readily - thus stengthening the role of membrane curvature.
- The response on individual filopodia often looks **all‑or‑none**, suggesting that Piezo1-Piezo1 or Yoda1-Piezo1 cooperative interactions may reinforce the curvature‑sorting change once a few channels flatten.
- The Yoda1 effect was also Ca<sup>2+</sup> - independent, as it was present in experiments done in Ca<sup>2+</sup>-free buffer. 
- The Yoda1 effect was also reversible, disappearing once Yoda1 was washed out.
