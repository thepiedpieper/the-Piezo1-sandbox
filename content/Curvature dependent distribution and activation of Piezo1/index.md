| **Yang et al. (2022)**  <br>_[[Distribution of Piezo1 is governed by membrane curvature\|Membrane curvature governs the distribution of Piezo1 in live cells]]_  <br>[_Nature_, 604, 377–383](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04574-8) | • Cryo‑electron tomography and live‑cell imaging were employed to demonstrate that Piezo1 is **enriched in regions of positive membrane curvature** in both artificial liposomes and native cell membranes.  <br>• The channel’s dome‑shaped architecture was shown to directly sense curvature; flattening of the dome under membrane tension reduces its curvature preference, allowing it to enter low‑curvature or negatively curved regions.  <br>• A curvature‑dependent partitioning coefficient was derived, consistent with a model where the intrinsic curvature of the closed channel matches that of highly curved membrane tubules (radii < 50 nm), whereas the open (flattened) channel is excluded from such tubules.  <br>• Depletion of Piezo1 from filopodia and membrane tethers was observed, and this exclusion was reversed by Yoda1‑induced flattening.  <br>• The physiological consequence of curvature sensing was demonstrated in red blood cells, where Piezo1 localization shifts between the high‑curvature rim and the low‑curvature dimple upon activation.                                                         | • This paper provides the **geometric basis** for Piezo1’s non‑uniform distribution: if the apical membrane possesses a different curvature spectrum (e.g., more microvilli, different membrane invaginations) than the basal membrane (which is dominated by flat adhesions and high tension), then Piezo1 will partition asymmetrically between the two.  <br>• The observation that Yoda1 overcomes curvature‑based exclusion implies that activation‑dependent flattening can redistribute Piezo1 along the Z‑axis if tension or activation state differs between the two sides. This supports the hypothesis that Piezo1’s apical–basal ratio is dynamically regulated by local mechanical cues.  <br>• The curvature‑sensing mechanism predicts that Piezo1 should be **depleted from highly curved apical protrusions** (e.g., blebs, microspikes) but enriched in flat, tense basal zones, a prediction directly testable by Z‑stack confocal analysis.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Dumitru et al. (2021)**  <br>_Probing PIEZO1 Localization upon Activation Using High‑Resolution Atomic Force and Confocal Microscopy_  <br>[_Nano Letters_, 21, 4076–4082](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.nanolett.1c00599)                | • High‑resolution AFM and confocal microscopy were combined to map the nanoscale distribution of Piezo1 on red blood cell (RBC) membranes in the resting and Yoda1‑activated states.  <br>• In resting RBCs, Piezo1 was found to be organized into **sub‑micrometric clusters** (~275 nm diameter), primarily located at the highly curved rim (high curvature, low tension).  <br>• Yoda1 treatment caused a **1.7‑fold increase in immunofluorescence intensity** and a significant increase in cluster number, specifically in the low‑curvature dimple region (low curvature, high tension), while cluster size remained constant.  <br>• Force‑distance AFM with PIEZO1‑antibody‑functionalized tips revealed that Yoda1 induces a **2‑fold increase in binding frequency** without altering the rupture force (157–159 pN), demonstrating increased epitope accessibility rather than a change in binding affinity.  <br>• These findings indicate that Piezo1 clusters are not static; channel activation exposes previously masked extracellular epitopes and enables redistribution of clusters to mechanically distinct membrane domains. | • This study is the closest precedent for your Z‑axis work. It directly demonstrates that **Piezo1 localization is not uniform** across a geometrically complex membrane and that **activation state modulates this distribution**.  <br>• The increase of Piezo1 in the dimple (low curvature, high tension) upon activation parallels your hypothesis: in adherent cells, the basal membrane is a low‑curvature, high‑tension environment (analogous to the RBC dimple), while the apical membrane may be higher curvature/lower tension (analogous to the RBC rim). Piezo1 should thus be enriched basally when the cell is under tension, and this enrichment should be sensitive to Yoda1.  <br>• The unchanged rupture force in AFM experiments confirms that activation does not alter the antibody–epitope bond strength—an important control for your own immunofluorescence‑based Z‑axis quantification, as it indicates that any observed change in apical/basal fluorescence after treatment genuinely reflects protein redistribution, not altered antibody binding.  <br>• The finding that cluster size remains constant while number increases suggests a mechanism of **cluster fission, fusion, or unmasking**, rather than de novo channel insertion. For your adherent cells, this implies that a shift in Piezo1 from apical to basal would likely involve lateral diffusion of pre‑existing clusters, not synthesis of new channels. |
