| **Zhao et al. (2018)**<br>*[[Structure and mechanogating mechanism of the Piezo1 channel]]*<br>[*Nature*, 554, 487–492](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature25743)                                                                                                                          | • A trimeric, propeller‑like architecture with three distal blades and a central ion‑conducting pore was resolved by cryo‑electron microscopy.<br>• The structure adopts a highly curved, dome‑like conformation in the closed state, with an in‑plane area of ~2400 nm².<br>• Gating is proposed to occur through a flattening of the dome upon mechanical force application, which directly opens the pore via a lever‑like mechanism.<br>• The large membrane footprint and intrinsic curvature render the channel exquisitely sensitive to changes in lateral membrane tension and curvature.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | • The physical basis for curvature‑ and tension‑dependent localization is established: the dome‑shaped closed state is energetically disfavoured in highly curved or low‑tension membrane domains, predicting its depletion from such regions unless activated.<br>• This implies that the basal membrane, which is under higher tension due to focal adhesions, may preferentially retain closed Piezo1, whereas the apical membrane, with lower tension, may disfavour it. Activation (e.g., by Yoda1) flattens the channel, altering this energetic preference and enabling redistribution.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |

                                                                               | • The inactivation kinetics of Piezo1 are shown to be directly modulated by the resting membrane tension of the channel’s sensitivity is not fixed but dynamically tuned by the local lipid composition and resting tension. | **Lüchtefeld et al. (2024)**<br>*[[Lüchtefeld 2024 \| Dissecting cell membrane tension dynamics and its effect on Piezo1‑mediated cellular mechanosensitivity using force‑controlled nanopipettes]]*<br>[*Nature Methods*, 21, 1063–1073](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02277-8) | • A fluidic force microscopy (FluidFM) platform is developed to independently apply indentation force and aspiration pressure while mapping local membrane tension via Flipper‑TR FLIM and recording Piezo1‑dependent Ca²⁺ responses.<br>• Membrane tension increases are demonstrated to be **locally confined** under mechanical stimulation in intact cells; long‑range propagation is observed only in cytoskeleton‑free blebs.<br>• The actin cortex acts as a mechanoprotective barrier, restricting tension spread and enabling spatial compartmentalization of mechanosensation.<br>• A linear relationship is established between indentation force and the aspiration pressure required to trigger Piezo1 (the pC,50(F) curve). The vertical shift of this curve reports resting membrane tension, while the slope reports cytoskeletal stiffness.<br>• Cytochalasin D (actin disruption) steepens the slope, whereas margaric acid (membrane‑stiffening) shifts the curve downward, decoupling membrane and cortex contributions to mechanosensitivity.<br>• The integrated membrane tension over the stimulated area, rather than peak tension alone, determines Piezo1 activation probability. | • This work provides the most direct evidence to date that the plasma membrane is mechanically compartmentalized by the actin cytoskeleton, creating distinct tension microdomains. It directly motivates a Z‑axis analysis: the basal side, with dense cortical actin and focal adhesions, should have a different tension landscape and Piezo1 activation profile than the apical side.<br>• The bleb experiment proves that tension propagates globally in the absence of the cortex; thus, any observed basal‑apical difference in Piezo1 distribution or activation is likely cytoskeleton‑dependent.<br>• A shift in Piezo1’s basal‑apical ratio after cytoskeletal or tension perturbation would be analogous to a change in the “Z‑axis response curve,” reflecting the altered mechanical state of each membrane compartment.<br>• The finding that activation depends on integrated tension over area reinforces the need to quantify not just the presence of Piezo1 but its local density and clustering, as these determine the functional output of a given tension input. |
